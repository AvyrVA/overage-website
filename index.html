What is a surplus?

The money the county is holding

Say you owed $40,000 on your property and it sold at auction for $105,000. The debt gets paid, the costs and fees get paid, and what’s left over sits with the court or the county. Depending on where you are, it’s called a surplus, an overage, or excess proceeds.

In most states, that money belongs to whoever owned the property when it sold, or to their heirs. But it does not come to you automatically. Someone has to file a claim, prove who’s entitled to it, and do it before the deadline runs out.

That’s the part we handle.

Your rights

Things we would rather you hear from us

A claim takes more than a form

Deed history, tax and sale records, probate and heirship paperwork, a court filing, and a deadline that does not move. We handle every step, and we advance every cost until your money is recovered.

The fee is a term, not a law of nature

Thirty percent is the most we charge, and on some matters it is negotiable downward. Ask. Anyone who tells you their rate is fixed and non-discussable is telling you about their sales process, not about your claim.

Finding out is free

You should not have to pay anyone to find out whether money is being held for you. Our case review costs nothing and obligates you to nothing.

There is a deadline

In Texas, a claim for excess proceeds from a tax sale generally has to be filed within two years of the sale. After that the funds are distributed to the taxing units. Foreclosure surpluses follow different rules, and your written case review gives the deadline for your sale.

No one legitimate will ask for this

No one legitimate will ask you for a wire to a personal account, a gift card, or your Social Security number before there is a signed agreement in place.

“Sign today” is pressure

If someone tells you the claim is lost unless you sign today, that is pressure, not information. The deadline is real; the urgency is manufactured.

Questions

The objections, answered flatly

Is this a scam?

It is the right question to ask. A legitimate recovery firm does not ask you for money before a recovery, does not ask you to sign over your property or your claim, and will tell you which sale and which fund it is talking about so you can check it yourself. We do all three. You can call the county or district clerk directly and confirm what we tell you. We encourage it.

How did you get my information?

From the public record. Foreclosure and tax sale results, and the excess proceeds held after them, are public documents. We read the same records anyone can request. Nothing came from a credit bureau, a bank, or a purchased list.

Why should I work with you?

Because a claim takes records, an attorney, filing costs, and someone watching the deadline. We handle all of it, we advance every cost, and we stay on the file until you’re paid. You pay nothing unless we recover your money.

What happens if you don’t recover anything?

You owe nothing. Not our time, not the costs, not the attorney. We advance all of it and we absorb it if the claim fails.

How long does it take?

Typically 2 to 5 months from filing to release of the funds. The court or the county sets the pace, and claims that run through an estate can take longer.
