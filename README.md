# Undistributed Refund Experts — website

Static site. No build step, no dependencies, no framework. Open `index.html` in
a browser to preview it exactly as it will deploy.

**The big correction of this session — read this before editing fee copy**

The old site claimed in sixteen places that the client never pays an attorney
fee ("Attorney fee — $0 to you", "no legal fee is charged to you"). The signed
fee agreement says the opposite: counsel fees are an **Expense**, reimbursed to
the firm out of the money recovered, deducted before the 30% is calculated. All
sixteen were corrected to match the agreement, as were five places claiming the
client may decline our attorney and retain their own — the agreement gives that
choice to the firm, not the client.

The fee is now stated everywhere as: **never more than 30%**, sometimes less,
negotiable downward only; third-party costs (including the attorney's fees)
reimbursed from the recovery first; nothing owed if nothing is recovered. The
$1,000 figure is not quoted anywhere, because the agreement sets expenses per
matter rather than at a fixed amount.

**If you change the fee agreement, change the site with it.** `PROPOSAL.md`
holds the full reasoning and the copy deck.

## Structure (rebuilt 18 Sep 2026)

Every page is generated by `tools/build.py`, so the header, nav, footer and
disclosures are identical everywhere. **Edit `tools/pages/*.html`, then run
`python3 tools/build.py`.** Don't hand-edit the HTML at the root; the next
build overwrites it.

```
.
├── index.html            Home: the full argument, in PROPOSAL.md section order
├── how-it-works.html     four steps in depth, what you receive, what signing commits you to
├── what-it-costs.html    the fee in full, what's included, alternatives
├── your-rights.html      who is entitled, Texas deadline, filing yourself, scam warnings
├── about.html            why you got a letter, who we are / are not, how to check us
├── faq.html              17 questions; FAQPage JSON-LD generated from the visible answers
├── start.html            intake form
├── terms.html · privacy.html · sms-terms.html
├── assets/
│   ├── site.css          the one stylesheet
│   ├── site.js           mobile nav + intake form (set INTAKE_ENDPOINT here)
│   ├── fonts/            Source Serif 4 + Public Sans, self-hosted woff2
│   └── img/              logo, favicon, illustrations, background patterns (all SVG)
├── tools/
│   ├── build.py          layout, icons, form, shared consent text
│   └── pages/            page sources
└── .gitignore            keeps the client agreement, PROPOSAL.md, ref/ and archive/ out of git
```

Nothing loads from a CDN or another domain. Fonts, icons and every
illustration are in the repo, and there is no stock photography.

To preview, serve the folder rather than double-clicking a file, because
browsers block local font loading from `file://`:

```bash
python3 -m http.server 8000   # then open http://localhost:8000
```

The SMS opt-in text lives once, in `SMS_CONSENT` in `tools/build.py`, and is
stamped into the form and into `sms-terms.html`, so the two can't drift.

## Before it goes live

Ordered by what actually blocks launch.

1. **The 30% fee is still unvalidated against statutory caps.** Texas caps fees
   on excess-proceeds claims (Tax Code ch. 34), and unclaimed property held by
   the Comptroller is capped lower still. Whether 30% plus reimbursed expenses
   clears the applicable cap depends on which fund holds the money. This can
   invalidate the fee copy on every page. Unanswered since the first session.

2. **The UPL structure needs counsel, not just wording.** The site now
   accurately says the firm selects and pays counsel and the client cannot
   substitute. That accuracy makes the unauthorized-practice question sharper:
   a non-lawyer entity retaining counsel for consumers implicates Tex. Gov't
   Code §81.101 et seq. and Disciplinary Rule 5.04. The cheapest fix is a
   one-line amendment to §1 of the fee agreement giving the claimant a
   substitution right — it restores the stronger copy and the safer structure
   at once.

3. **Two documents contradict each other.** The mandated footer disclosure says
   the client "may stop doing business with us at any time" and owes nothing if
   they reject an offer. The agreement's non-circumvention clause says the full
   percentage becomes payable, by wire within two business days, if they pursue
   the claim themselves after signing. Both cannot govern the same engagement.
   If the disclosure is required for this service line, the clause is what has
   to change. Flagged in `terms.html` §7 and in `PROPOSAL.md`.

4. **There is no cancellation or rescission clause in the agreement at all.**
   Not "you can't cancel" — the document simply doesn't address it.
   Recommendation: three business days. It costs little and is the cheapest
   trust-builder available to the site.

5. **Legal pages are counsel drafts.** `privacy.html`, `terms.html`,
   `sms-terms.html` each carry a visible draft banner and dashed-bronze blocks
   marking decisions only your attorney or the business can make. Terms §7 and
   §8 were rewritten this session to match the fee agreement and are
   **unreviewed**. Get all three read, fill the bracketed values, then delete
   the `<div class="draftbanner">` block from each.

6. **The form has no endpoint.** GitHub Pages is static and can't receive a
   POST. Set `INTAKE_ENDPOINT` at the top of `assets/site.js`. Until then the form
   opens the visitor's email app with their details filled in, addressed to
   the intake mailbox, and says so on screen. See "Wiring up the form" below.

7. **Other states.** The site no longer claims Texas-only, but it doesn't claim
   anywhere else either — the footer lists Texas and invites out-of-state
   enquiries. Before adding a state: its excess-proceeds deadline and
   redemption window on `your-rights.html` (counsel-verified, as Texas is), the
   footer jurisdiction list, and any state-specific fee cap or licensing
   requirement. Several states regulate this business separately.

8. **No canonical URL or `og:url`.** The homepage has `og:title`,
   `og:description` and an Organization schema block, but no canonical tag and
   no absolute URL anywhere. Add both once the domain is known.

## Editing the statutory text

Any element with `class="statutory"` is mandated disclosure text — Reg O, FTC
typicality, TCPA/10DLC, and UPL. Don't paraphrase it, shorten it, set it below
13px, drop it below 4.5:1 contrast, or move it inside anything collapsed. The
reasoning for each block is in a comment directly above it. Four blocks were
rewritten this session for the attorney-fee correction and are unreviewed — see
blocker 5.

The SMS opt-in language in `sms-terms.html` is character-for-character identical
to the checkbox in `start.html`. Keep it that way — a mismatch is a common A2P
10DLC rejection reason. If you edit one, edit both.

## Wiring up the form

Point the form at a hosted endpoint — Formspree, Basin, Netlify Forms, or your
own. Whatever you use must record the originating IP alongside the consent
fields the page already captures (exact checkbox text as rendered, ISO
timestamp, page URL, user agent, form version), reject submissions whose
consent flags weren't set by user action, and store the payload encrypted at
rest. A TCPA consent defense is only as good as the record of what the consumer
actually saw.

SMS consent is optional and never gates submission. Don't change that without
counsel — the reasoning is in a comment above the checkbox.

## Design rules carried forward

- Palette follows the reference in `ref/`: deep teal, gold, sage, off-white.
  Disclosure blocks use a teal rule at body size and full contrast, so they
  read as part of the argument rather than fine print.
- No urgency theater: no countdown timers, no scarcity copy, no animated
  counters, no "act now." The deadline is real; manufacturing pressure around
  it reads as predatory and invites regulatory attention.
- No "cash for houses" register: no stock photography of handshakes, families,
  or sold signs. No exclamation points.
- Light theme only, deliberately. An institutional financial-services page
  should look the same for every visitor, and `color-scheme: light` keeps
  native inputs and scrollbars light for visitors whose OS prefers dark.
- No unsourced testimonials, and no invented statistics. The homepage proof row
  deliberately shows facts ($0 upfront, 30% max, licensed attorney, since 2021)
  rather than claimed metrics, because a fabricated number is the fastest way
  to lose a skeptical reader who checks.

## Deploy to GitHub Pages

Not a git repository yet. To deploy:

```bash
git init
git add .
git commit -m "Surplus recovery site"
git branch -M main
git remote add origin https://github.com/USER/REPO.git
git push -u origin main
```

Then: **Settings → Pages → Source: Deploy from a branch → `main` / `/ (root)`**.
Live in about a minute at `https://USER.github.io/REPO/`.

Custom domain: add a `CNAME` file containing just the domain, point a DNS
`CNAME` record at `USER.github.io`, then tick **Enforce HTTPS** in Settings →
Pages once the certificate provisions.

**Don't `git add .` without pruning first.** These are in the working directory
and should not be published:

- The client fee agreement (`Fee_Agreement_*.doc`). It identifies a real client
  and must never go into a public repo.
- `ref/` — four competitors' saved homepages and screenshots.
- `archive/` — previous revisions, kept for comparison.
- `Screenshot 2026-09-03 … .png`, `prompt for fable.txt` — working files.
- `PROPOSAL.md` — internal; it discusses fee structure and legal exposure.

A `.gitignore` covering those is the safer move than remembering each time.
